# MedBhasha — Data Collection Rules
### Version 1.0 · Released [October 12, 2026]

Participants source and annotate their own clinical text — data collection is
itself part of the task, and the part with the most risk if left unguided.
**Read this in full before you collect a single note.** These rules are
mandatory, apply to every team, and are enforced at submission review.

---

## 1. Approved sources

You may collect clinical or health-related text from:

- **Publicly published, de-identified case reports** — e.g., open-access
  journal case reports, PubMed Central articles
- **Synthetic clinical notes generated by your own team** — e.g., via an LLM
  prompted to mimic Bengali clinical documentation style
- **Existing open, licensed medical text or NLP datasets**, used with proper
  attribution in your source declaration
- **Publicly available, de-identified transcription samples**
- **Information collected directly from a consenting patient, about their
  own health** — this is a special, more heavily governed category. If you
  want to use it, read §1a in full before collecting anything this way.

If you're unsure whether a source qualifies, open a GitHub issue and ask
before you annotate it — not after.

---

## 1a. Direct patient-reported health information (special category)

Collecting information directly from a patient, about their own health, with
their informed consent, is allowed — but only under the conditions below.
This is the **only** route by which real (non-synthetic) individual health
information may enter this hackathon. **Real hospital EHR data, hospital
records, or any data obtained through access to a hospital's systems is
never permitted, under any circumstance (see §2) — this exception does not
create a path to that data.**

**Before collecting anything this way, your team must:**

1. **Use the official consent form (`consent_form.pdf`) exactly as provided.**
   Do not modify, shorten, or verbally paraphrase it instead of using it. It
   must be read aloud (or read by the patient themselves) and signed
   **before** any information is collected.
2. **Confirm every eligibility condition on the form before proceeding:**
   the patient is 18 or older, is able to give informed consent (not in an
   emergency, not in acute distress, not pressured by anyone), and the form
   has been presented in a language they're comfortable with. If any
   condition fails, do not collect from that person.
3. **Collect only what the patient volunteers in their own words.** Your
   team is not a clinician — do not probe for a clinical history beyond what
   the patient chooses to share, and do not attempt to diagnose, assess, or
   advise them.
4. **Anonymize before annotation.** Remove or generalize the patient's name,
   exact age, exact location, workplace, relationship details, or any
   combination of details specific enough to identify them — not just
   obvious identifiers like name and phone number. If you're unsure whether
   a detail is identifying, remove it or ask the clinical advisor.
5. **Retain the signed consent form yourselves.** It must be available to
   organizers on request, but must **never** be included in your dataset
   submission, your EDA, or the public release — the consent form itself
   contains identifying information that the released text must not.
6. **Respect withdrawal.** If a patient withdraws consent before your final
   submission, delete their information from your corpus entirely before
   submitting.

Teams using this category must note it explicitly in their source
declaration (see §3), including how many notes came from this route — this
is reported in aggregate only, never in a way that could be traced back to
an individual.

---

## 2. Never permitted, under any circumstance

- **Real hospital EHR data or exports**, in any form
- **Any text containing a real patient's name, ID, date of birth, address,
  or other identifying detail** — even if the rest of the note is otherwise
  usable
- **Scraped social media content describing an identifiable real person's
  medical situation**

There is no exception to this list. A single violation can result in removal
of the affected notes from scoring and the public dataset release;
deliberate or repeated violations disqualify the team (see §5).

---

## 3. Process: trust, then verify

Sources are **not** pre-approved before you start annotating. Every
submission is checked afterward. This only works if every team follows the
process honestly:

1. **Submit a source declaration** alongside your annotated corpus,
   describing where every note came from and which source category (§1) it
   falls under. **Submissions without a source declaration are not scored.**
2. **Disclose your annotation method** in the same declaration — rule-based/
   classical tagging, built-in NER tools, LLM-assisted annotation, manual
   tagging, or a mix. Any method is acceptable; non-disclosure is not.

---

## 4. Exploratory Data Analysis (required)

Because you're sourcing your own data, basic EDA is how you check its
quality before you submit, not just a reporting formality. Your Round 1
submission must include an EDA summary covering:

- Entity frequency distribution across your corpus
- Note-length distribution
- Basic relationship/co-occurrence patterns between entity types

This is scored separately in Round 1 judging and also functions as your own
early warning system — a corpus that's accidentally skewed toward one entity
type or one note style will usually show up here before anyone else looks
at it.

---

## 5. Consequences

| Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|
| A note traced to a real, non-de-identified patient | Removed from scoring and from the public dataset release |
| Patient-collected data used without the official consent form, or with any eligibility condition unmet | Those notes removed from scoring and release; repeated instances treated as a rule violation under §5 below |
| Consent form (or any identifying detail from it) found inside a submission | Submission flagged for review; affected notes removed |
| Missing or incomplete source declaration | Submission not scored |
| Pattern of carelessness (e.g., repeated near-identical synthetic notes passed off as diverse data) | Flagged in Diversity/Quality scoring; may affect Round 1 score significantly |
| Deliberate or repeated rule violations | Team disqualification |

Decisions on consequences are made by the organizing team and are final.

---

## 6. Consent

By registering, your team consents that your annotated corpus may be
included in a public, open-licensed dataset release (e.g., CC-BY-4.0) after
the event, and in a potential publication, with contributor acknowledgment.
MIMIC-derived or any other restricted-access artifacts are not part of this
hackathon and must never appear in your submission.
